    How the limits were worked out
      Values for each sample

      How to read this

      Each dot is one sample. The solid line is the process average and the red dashed lines are the control limits, three standard deviations either side.

      A dot outside the limits, or 7 dots in a row on one side of the average, means something unusual (a special cause) has affected the process. Dots moving around inside the limits are normal common-cause variation, so don't react to each one.

      Try this in class

      • Change one value in sample 8 to 210 and watch both charts.
      • Ask the class: if the process is in control, does every packet meet the customer's specification? Control limits come from the process; specification limits come from the customer.

      Your data

      One cause per line: the name, a comma (or tab), then the count.

      Table of causes

      How to read this

      Bars show how often each cause happens, largest first. The red line is the running total as a percentage of everything counted.

      The causes up to where the line passes 80% are the vital few. Juran's point: fixing these few gives most of the improvement, so start there instead of spreading effort across the trivial many.

      Try this in class

      • Ask everyone what slows down their canteen order. Tally the answers on the board, type them in and read the chart together.
      • Then ask: is the most frequent cause always the most costly? A rare cause can still matter most.

      Causes for each bone

      One cause per line. Start a line with * to mark a likely root cause; it turns red. The boxes are laid out like the diagram: top bones first, then bottom bones.

      How to read this

      The head of the fish is the problem. Each bone is a group of possible causes. For every cause, keep asking "why?" until you reach something the team can act on.

      This is the Plan step of PDCA: understand the causes before choosing a fix.

      Try this in class

      • Split the class into six groups, one per bone. Give each group five minutes, then fill in the diagram together.
      • Ask each group to mark one root cause with * and defend it.
